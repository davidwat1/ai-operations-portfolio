Scaling the businessGrowth · Capacity · Leadership depth · Commercial discipline

My perspective

At Experis, I led a volatile, seasonal services business that had to scale capacity aggressively in both directions. My challenge was building flexibility without losing delivery and commercial control. I was accountable for a roughly $50M P&L, delivery quality and customer commitments while building a leadership structure that could operate at greater scale.

What would you like to explore next?

How did I lead through it?

I led through eight senior leaders rather than pulling every decision upward. I set the business's delivery goals and expectations, established clear accountability, and used recurring operating reviews to keep everyone aligned. That gave my leaders the authority to make decisions while remaining accountable for the results.

What operating decisions did I make?

I used biweekly workforce forecasting and monthly and quarterly business reviews to connect client demand, capacity planning, delivery risks and commercial commitments.

What changed—and what can I substantiate?

I led an organization of approximately 1,000 people at scale, helped launch delivery centers in Milwaukee and Budapest, added Meta as a client, negotiated an exclusive services arrangement with Amazon, and expanded compatibility testing, data analytics and telemetry, and mobile game testing readiness services. Headcount varied with seasonal demand.

Restoring customer confidenceEscalations · Service recovery · Accountability

My perspective

At Experis, I faced a situation where most delivery KPIs were being met, but the customer was dissatisfied. Unannounced resource changes and unclear expectations of customer-facing roles had damaged trust. I needed to address the experience behind the metrics.

What would you like to explore next?

How did I lead through it?

I personally led a formal corrective action plan. I made it clear that customer confidence was a leadership responsibility, not simply a reporting issue.

What operating decisions did I make?

I introduced advance discussions about proposed resource changes, clarified customer-facing role expectations and strengthened incident tracking and reporting.

What changed—and what can I substantiate?

Over approximately three months, customer-assigned communication ratings improved from 5/10 to 8/10 and leadership ratings from 6/10 to 9/10. These were two specific rating dimensions, not an overall CSAT index.

Improving operational efficiencyCost discipline · Quality · Repeatable execution

My perspective

At Microsoft, I led Xbox Certification at Principal level, with approximately $30M in annual operating expense and more than 1,000 submissions annually. The challenge was to improve the operating model while maintaining the quality and predictability expected of a platform certification service.

Turning AI and technology into accountable executionAutomation · Governance · Evidence · Human oversight

My perspective

I see AI as a way to improve how work is performed, but only when the underlying process is clear. In my own projects, I have been applying the disciplines I used in services leadership to workflows that combine automation, software engineering and human decisions.

What would you like to explore next?

How did I lead through it?

I define decision rights, approval boundaries and exception ownership before treating an automated step as reliable. I remain accountable for consequential decisions and factual claims.

What operating decisions did I make?

I structure intake, qualification, evidence checks, governed action, reconciliation and reporting as a repeatable operating process. I also use testing and quality gates in software projects.

What changed—and what can I substantiate?

I can show documented designs, workflow configuration and technical artifacts. I am not claiming measured enterprise AI ROI, fully automated end-to-end reliability or production-scale outcomes.
