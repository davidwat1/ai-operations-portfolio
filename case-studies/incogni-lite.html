Early-stage concept · Privacy operations

Incogni-Lite: making personal-data removal requests manageable and auditable.

I see privacy requests as a recurring operational challenge, not just a series of emails: identify the broker, prepare the right request, track acknowledgment, monitor deadlines and preserve evidence of the outcome.

The problem

Personal-data opt-out and deletion requests are fragmented across providers and difficult to monitor consistently.

The design direction

A request registry, templates, email workflow, status dashboard and exportable history.

The maturity boundary

Early design and implementation were discussed, but current source and working end-to-end behavior have not been independently verified.

Proposed operating model

Request lifecycle

Identify broker → prepare request → send or submit → monitor response → verify disposition.

Data model

Python and SQLite were considered for broker records, request states, dates and audit history.

Communication workflow

Reusable privacy request templates and Microsoft Graph email integration were part of the planned approach.

Management view

An HTML dashboard, status reporting and exports would surface outstanding and completed requests.

Design decisions and safeguards

I believe privacy workflows need more than automation. I would design controls around authority to submit requests, protection of sensitive information, duplicate prevention, verification of deletion and exception records. These are design requirements, not claims that the controls have been implemented.

Why it belongs in the portfolio

With Incogni-Lite, I am exploring an early-stage operating-design pattern: translating a recurring compliance-oriented problem into a defined workflow with ownership, state tracking and evidence. It is shown as a concept, not a released privacy service. The relevant leadership capability is the translation of a high-friction process into clear states, controls, ownership and measurable outcomes.
