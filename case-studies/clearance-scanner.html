ClearanceScanner: from bargain detection to evidence-driven retail intelligence.

A discount is not a decision. ClearanceScanner explores how to turn inconsistent local retailer signals into traceable observations, confidence-aware rankings and actions that users can evaluate.

The problem

Clearance opportunities are local, time-sensitive and hard to verify. A price signal without provenance or inventory confidence can mislead.

The response

Build a modular Python platform for acquisition, observation history, scoring, replay, testing and operational governance.

The evidence boundary

Historical package validations and tests are documented. Live retailer accuracy, reliable inventory coverage and production deployment are not independently established.

Product decision loop

01 · Find02 · Verify03 · Rank04 · Explain05 · Act

The operating principle is to preserve the evidence behind each opportunity rather than present an opaque deal score. The product is deliberately designed to distinguish an observed price, an inferred opportunity and a verified in-store outcome.

Engineering system

Data and history

SQLite persistence, observation records, price-change history, replay datasets and evidence lineage support repeatable analysis.

Decision intelligence

Scoring, confidence, ranking and explainability separate promising opportunities from weak or incomplete signals.

Quality engineering

Automated tests, deterministic fixtures, regression validation and scenario replay were central to the development approach.

Operational readiness

Deployment reproducibility, telemetry, rollback, runtime governance and connector validation were treated as explicit workstreams.

Documented development progression

Earlier project records show an MVP with a mock Walmart scanner, local scoring, reporting and dashboard. Subsequent packages expanded into observation history, evidence management, deployment validation and retailer integration frameworks. A preserved May 2026 project bootstrap identifies a pre-deployment hardening phase, approximately 87 Python files, 38 test files and more than 300 test functions. Those are historical inventory estimates supplied by the project record, not a fresh independent audit or passing-test count.

Leadership and product decisions

Through this work, I am exploring how to set product direction, translate ambiguity into engineering requirements, preserve traceability and make reliability part of the roadmap rather than an afterthought. It also demonstrates an important restraint: simulation, deterministic replay and passing local tests do not prove retailer integration quality.

Why this matters

ClearanceScanner demonstrates the discipline required to move from a useful prototype toward a trustworthy operating product: define the decision, preserve provenance, measure confidence, design failure handling and separate simulated success from live-world validity.
